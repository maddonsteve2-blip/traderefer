## Distilled Notes

Caleb reacts to a case study claiming a local ranking spike came from a sudden Google review burst. He accepts that review campaigns can create a visibility lift, but argues the durable ranking signal is usually user behavior and goal completion, not the review spike itself.

Important takeaways:

- Reviews help conversion and AI visibility, but reviews alone do not replace service/location architecture.
- A ranking spike can expose a business to users long enough for calls, hires, and no-bounce outcomes to reinforce the ranking.
- Local rank grids can make a business look dominant if the search radius or keyword framing is too narrow.
- AI systems increasingly read review text for attributes, services, outcomes, and user situations.
- Specific reviews are more useful than generic reviews. Asking customers to describe what happened creates stronger content for AI and local matching.
- For city-wide map visibility, businesses still need entity strength, correct categories, service architecture, and location pages that genuinely serve the claimed area.

## TradeRefer Translation

TradeRefer cannot manufacture Google review velocity for listed businesses, and should not pretend to. But it can make profile pages more useful by surfacing the specific trust data it already has:

- business specialties,
- service areas,
- visible review/referral signals,
- project examples if available,
- claims or verification status,
- plain-language outcome descriptions.

For AI SEO, the goal is to help an assistant answer: "Which local trades can help with this specific job in this suburb, and why should I trust them?"

## Actions For TradeRefer

- Add "best fit for" or "services this profile appears to cover" blocks only when backed by real business data.
- Encourage claimed businesses to add specific service descriptions and real job examples.
- Track zero-click top-ranking profiles as CTR opportunities, not just ranking wins.
- Avoid claims like "trusted", "best", or "verified" unless TradeRefer has evidence.
- Build a prompt/query test set around attributes users actually ask for: emergency, licensed, nearby, residential, commercial, specific job type, suburb, availability.

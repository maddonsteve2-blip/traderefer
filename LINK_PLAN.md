# TradeRefer.au — 90-Day Link-Building Plan

Execution layer under `SEO_RECOVERY_PLAN.md` Phase 2 ("Links... THE fix", target 30-50
referring domains in 90 days). Doctrine source: `link-building.md` +
`contradiction-ledger.md` in `~/.claude/skills/general-seo/references/`, read in full
before this was written — risk labels below are carried from those files, not invented.

**Ground truth:** ~4.5 months old, **1 referring domain total** (DataForSEO) — the
binding constraint. De-indexed to ~2-3 Google pages after a launch noindex accident;
recovery machinery (2,284-URL curated sitemap, googlebot gating, 2 Omega Indexer test
batches reading out ~Aug 12-19) is already live and out of scope here. Bing healthy
(1-2k impressions/day) and must not be disturbed. Solo founder, Geelong VIC, limited
budget, **cannot afford to lose the domain** → no PBNs, no parasite placements, on
this domain, ever (ledger §4/§9: "never attach a brand or client domain's fate to
them" — absolute given the constraint, not a judgment call).

**The one adaptation that matters most:** treat traderefer.au as Google-authority-zero
starting today, not as a 4.5-month-old site — 1 RD is a brand-new-site link profile
regardless of calendar age. Everything below maps onto Indexsy's 12-month arc
(`link-building.md` §2) from month 0, and per ledger §9 defaults to Indexsy's *slower*
plan throughout, not Diggity's faster one — this is a first-timer/recovery profile,
not an established one with vetting discipline already built up.

## 2. Anchor-text strategy

Doctrine (§3, §14.4): exact-match on a young/recovering domain is "the fastest route
to a penalty" and "the classic clap." No competitor-average benchmarking yet (§14.4
needs an existing profile to average — there isn't one at 1 RD); revisit that method
once past ~20-30 RDs (day 60+, pull via `dfs` against the top-ranking AU trade
directories to calibrate months 4-12).

| Bucket | Target share (blended, months 1-3) | Sources |
|---|---|---|
| Branded ("TradeRefer", "TradeRefer.au") | 45-55% | Citations, badge copy, guest posts |
| Naked URL (`traderefer.au`, `https://traderefer.au`) | 25-30% | Citations, one new badge style (gap — see §4), guest posts |
| Generic/CTA ("trades directory", "compare local tradies," "find a tradie") | 15-20% | Badge copy, guest post bios |
| Longtail exact/partial (e.g. "Australian trade job cost index," "compare tradie quotes online") | ≤5% | Digital PR only + max 1-2 deliberate niche-edit placements. Never on badge links. |
| True exact-match commercial ("Geelong plumber," "best electrician Melbourne") | 0% | None, full stop, for the whole 90 days |

Rule from §14.4 point 4 carried forward: never reuse the identical anchor string twice
where you control the copy. This is why §4 below expands the badge program from 3 fixed
strings to a rotating set — at hundreds of installs, 3 fixed strings *is* reuse at scale.

## 4. Claim-badge loop — the tier-1 scalable asset

This is the only channel that can go from 1 RD to 30-50+ without professional PR
spend, because it doesn't need journalists to say yes — it needs claimed tradies to
paste HTML. That's also exactly why it needs the tightest safety governor in this
plan: it is structurally a *widget distributed across many third-party sites*, the
pattern link-spam detection is built to catch, and the current build (verified in
`apps/web/app/dashboard/business/badge/page.tsx`) has two real gaps worth closing
before scaling send volume:

**Gap 1 — anchor variation.** All 3 existing badge styles ("Verified Partner," "Proud
partner of TradeRefer — Refer a customer, earn $X →," "Become a Referrer →") are
fixed strings, and **none produce a naked-URL anchor** — the whole badge program
today outputs zero links in that bucket (see §2 table). Fix: add a 4th, minimal style
whose visible text is the bare domain (`traderefer.au`), and 2-3 more phrasing
variants within the existing branded/generic buckets (e.g. "Find us on TradeRefer —
Australia's trade referral directory," "See our TradeRefer profile & reviews," "Refer
a customer to {business_name} via TradeRefer" — the last one is naturally unique per
install since it interpolates the business name). Assign each business a variant by a
stable hash of its slug so the same business always gets the same copy — deterministic,
not randomized per pageview, so nothing looks manipulative on inspection.

**Gap 2 — dofollow/nofollow mix.** All 3 current styles are fully dofollow
(`rel="noopener"`, no `nofollow`). At scale that's hundreds of identical-template
dofollow links appearing on a domain that has exactly 1 RD today — the precise
overnight-velocity spike §9/§14.3 warn kills new/recovering sites ("1,000 links in
month one is never natural"). Fix, using fields already on `BusinessData`
(`is_verified`, `avg_rating`, `total_reviews` — no new schema needed):
- **Default = `rel="nofollow noopener"`** for every fresh claim. This is the
  self-serve, zero-manual-vetting tier — it should carry the lowest risk profile.
- **Dofollow unlocks automatically** once a profile is `is_verified = true` AND
  `avg_rating ≥ 4.0` AND `total_reviews ≥ 5` — real-world reputation earned before
  TradeRefer, not something gamed same-day. Expect a meaningful chunk of claims to
  qualify immediately (scraped profiles carry pre-existing Google review data), which
  is fine — the gate is what matters, not a manufactured quota.
- Nofollow links still get crawled and still carry brand-mention/traffic value (§1);
  this isn't a lesser link, it's a safer default for a domain that can't take the hit.

**Velocity governor (ties to §1 table).** Don't send all 23,850 claim emails at once.
Month 1 = 150-300 (pilot), month 2 = ramp to 1,000-1,500/week, month 3 = scale off
observed claim rate. At a plausible 2-5% claim rate this alone produces the bulk of
the 30-50 RD target without ever looking like a single-day spike.

**Compliance (already flagged in `SEO_RECOVERY_PLAN.md`):** frame claim emails as a
listing-notification/claim-notice under the AU Spam Act (factual — "your business is
listed, here's how to claim it"), not marketing; honour every unsubscribe/removal.

**Destination check (1-line dev task, not in scope here):** badges currently link to
`/b/{slug}/refer?src=badge` (the referrer-signup funnel), not `/b/{slug}` (the profile
page that's actually in the curated Google sitemap once claimed). Confirm `/refer`
internally links back to `/b/{slug}` so equity still reaches the indexable page —
don't re-architect the funnel to chase this, just verify the internal link exists.

**Measurement:** `handleCopy` today only fire-and-forget logs `badge_style` on
copy-click — it doesn't confirm the badge ever went live. A monthly crawl-back check
(fetch each claimed business's known site, grep for the `referUrl` pattern — or a
`dfs` backlink pull) turns "copied" into a real installed-badge count for tracking
against the targets in §8.

## 5. Digital PR data play — Australian Trade Job Cost Index

Doctrine §5: assets that earn are original data studies; §1: brand mentions from
digital PR are "the AI-era complement to links" and land both at once. The honest
framing per doctrine: a $5k professional campaign targets 5-10 links (§5) — this is
the $0, DIY, solo-founder version, so the realistic 90-day expectation is 0-3 earned
placements, not a guaranteed hit. Run it anyway — it compounds every month it exists,
and it's genuinely differentiated: **160 canonical materials, a growing Bunnings-scraped
retail catalog, and per-job cost breakdowns across 148 job pages** is a real dataset
nobody else in AU trades has published as a standalone asset.

**Asset page to build:** a new page (e.g. `/trade-cost-index`) — nothing like it
exists in `apps/web/app/(public)` today. Reuse the existing freshness pattern already
built into `JobMaterialsCard.tsx` (`sampled_at` → "Sampled [Month Year]"), reuse the
existing `scripts/materials/ingest-prices.mjs` pipeline for monthly re-sampling (no
new data infrastructure needed). Contents: a ranked table of the top 15-20 tracked
jobs by material cost, a methodology footnote (retailer: Bunnings; N materials
tracked; sample size), and one shareable chart image watermarked "Source:
traderefer.au/trade-cost-index" (§5's Reddit-replication tactic: journalists link the
graphic's stated source directly).

**Pitch angles (truthful, from the real DB only — never fabricate a stat):**
1. Cost-of-renovating tracker — month-on-month material cost movement for the top
   10 jobs (bathroom reno, deck build, switchboard upgrade, hot water replacement) —
   cost-of-living angle.
2. Materials-vs-labour cost split per job type — "where does your reno dollar go,"
   a proven shareable chart format.
3. Seasonal cost spikes (hot water systems pre-winter, decking pre-summer) tied to
   real seasonal search demand.
Do **not** attempt a state-by-state cost comparison unless the underlying retail data
actually carries store-level regional pricing — don't manufacture a split the dataset
doesn't support.

**Target publications:** news.com.au (finance/reno), realestate.com.au (Renovate),
Domain.com.au, Canstar Blue, Finder.com.au (cost-of-living data desk), Houzz AU,
Better Homes and Gardens Australia, smartcompany.com.au (founder-story + data angle),
Yahoo Finance Australia, and — the highest-probability hit — the **Geelong
Advertiser** (local-founder-builds-national-data-tool is a genuinely easy local pitch).
Track in a spreadsheet per §5: name, publication, their article, a similar article,
template + customized portion per email.

**Press release rule (ledger §8):** if you put this out on a newswire, no anchor
links, ever — brand mention / naked URL only. Newswires are an AI-ingestion channel,
not a link-building one.

## 7. What NOT to do (risk labels carried from doctrine)

- **PBN links or PBN-farmed citations** — ledger §4/§9: "disposable-asset technology…
  never attach a brand or client domain's fate to them." Domain can't be lost. **Excluded, not optional.**
- **Parasite SEO** (sponsored posts on Medium/LinkedIn Pulse/news-site "partner
  content" pointing at money pages) — §12: site-reputation abuse, host domain "gets
  clapped in waves"; practitioner advice during any crackdown window is "sit on your
  hands." **Excluded, not optional.**
- **Fiverr $5 link packages for money pages** — §11: "worthless-to-harmful." The only
  legitimate $5-tier spend is the named citations in §3.
- **Exact-match commercial anchors** ("Geelong plumber," "best electrician Melbourne")
  in months 1-3 — §2/§3: "the classic clap" on a young/recovering domain.
- **Subdirectory/money-page link targets** before month 9 of the arc — out of scope
  for this 90-day window entirely (§2).
- **Mass same-day badge rollout** (all 23,850 claim emails at once) — this plan's own
  adapted risk (§4/§9/§14.3): would replicate the exact overnight-spike footprint
  that gets new domains manually reviewed.
- **Optional, explicitly risk-labeled, low-risk-to-domain (§11 "authority
  cosmetics"):** legitimate small-business/local-business award programs (self-nominate
  or apply) that grant a winner/finalist badge + link. Doctrine's own caveat: "awards
  can be pay-to-play (engines can't tell — yet)." Skip if time-constrained in the
  first 90 days; revisit month 4+ if the Chamber of Commerce relationship (§3)
  surfaces a legitimate regional business-awards program.
